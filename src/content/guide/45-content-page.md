# Content Pipeline
<!-- part: Making things | order: 45 | slug: content-page | route: /content | tags: content, content pipeline, content items, list, kanban, stages, formats, hook, cta, keyword, affiliate link, linked product, linked tasks, publish kit, automation runs, approval -->

The Content screen, headed "Content Pipeline", is the running list of content pieces for one project, from first idea to published. Use it to add pieces, move them through their stages, and attach the follow-up tasks that go with each one.

## Where to find it
- On a computer, tap "Content" under "Content & Production" in the left menu.
- On a phone, tap "More" in the bottom bar, then "Content" under "Content & Production".
- On a brand's page, "View all" on the "Content pipeline" section opens this screen for that brand.
- The screen shows the project chosen in the project picker at the top of the screen. With "All projects" chosen, it says "Select a channel to view its content pipeline."

## What you see
- The heading "Content Pipeline" with the project's name, and a "New Content Item" button.
- A tip box titled "Content Pipeline — 7 stages". Its stage list is out of date; you can close it with its X.
- The real stages, in order: "Idea", "Script", "Design", "Record", "Edit", "Thumbnail", "Scheduled" and "Published".
- The "List" tab: a table with "Title", "Format", "Stage" (a dropdown you can change right in the row), "Due", "Flags" ("Repurposed", "Monetized"), and edit and delete buttons. On a phone, swipe it sideways.
- The "Kanban" tab: one column per stage with a count. Each card shows the title, format, flags, due date, and an arrow button naming the next stage.

## How to use it
### Add a content piece
1. Tap "New Content Item".
2. Type a "Title". It is the only required field.
3. Pick a "Format" (Long-form, Short, Livestream, Community Post, Article, Carousel, Pin, Email, Reel, Post, Story or Printable) and a "Stage". A new piece starts as Long-form at Idea.
4. Fill in what helps: "Hook", "CTA" (call to action), "Primary Keyword", "Affiliate Link", "Linked Product", "Due Date", "Scheduled", "Published", "Repurposed" and "Monetized".
5. Tap "Create".

### Move a piece to its next stage
1. In "List", change the "Stage" dropdown on the piece's row.
2. Or, in "Kanban", tap the arrow button on its card to move it one column on.
3. If HQ refuses the move, a message says why (see Good to know).

### Edit a piece and attach tasks
1. Tap the pencil on its row in "List", or tap its card in "Kanban".
2. Change any field and tap "Save".
3. Under "Linked Tasks", type a "New task title", pick a "Priority" and a "Due" date, and tap "Add". It becomes a normal HQ task tied to this piece.
4. Under "Automation Runs", see any batch of tasks HQ made for this piece, and tap "Undo" to remove a batch.

### Delete a piece
1. In "List", tap the trash button on the piece's row.
2. Confirm "Delete this content item?". This can't be undone.

## Words on this screen
- Hook: the opening line or angle meant to grab attention.
- CTA: call to action, the thing you ask the viewer to do next.
- Primary Keyword: the main search phrase the piece aims at.
- Affiliate Link: an affiliate web address for this piece, typed in by hand.
- Linked Product: a product from the Products screen that this piece promotes.
- Repurposed and Monetized: labels you tick for a piece made from another piece, or a piece that earns money.
- Long-form publish kit: the batch of follow-up tasks HQ makes when a long-form piece is published.

## Good to know
- The first time a Long-form piece moves to "Published", HQ creates eight follow-up tasks: five "Create Short" tasks, a blog post, an email, and a review of the affiliate call to action. They appear under "Linked Tasks" and as "Long-form publish kit" under "Automation Runs". "Undo" deletes them; if you later move the piece out of "Published" and back in, HQ makes a new batch.
- For your own brands (not client projects), HQ will not let a piece move to "Scheduled" or "Published" until it is approved, and shows a message such as "Cannot move to scheduled until the item is approved". Approval happens on the Production Board or the brand's "Content" tab once the production work is finished, whatever the quality score.
- The Kanban arrow always moves a piece to the next column in the fixed order, even when that stage doesn't fit the format; a video at "Script" moves to "Design". Use the "Stage" dropdown to jump to the right stage.
- There is no drag and drop on the Kanban, even though the tip box mentions it.
- A piece whose stage is not one of the eight sits in the "Idea" column on the Kanban, and its "Stage" dropdown in "List" may look blank.
- "Scheduled" and "Published" are dates you record. This screen does not post anything for you.
- Radar briefs, and ideas made with "Make this" in the Answer Bank, also appear in this list for their brand.
- On a phone the Kanban columns stack one under another, so scroll down to reach the later stages.

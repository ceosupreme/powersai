# Glossary of HQ words
<!-- part: Reference | order: 96 | slug: glossary | route: none | tags: glossary, words, terms, status words, definitions, meaning, what does it mean -->

HQ uses the same words on many screens. This glossary explains each one in plain English, grouped by topic, so a word means the same thing wherever you see it. Words in quotes appear on the screen exactly as written.

## Projects and brands

- Project: anything HQ tracks, whether it is one of your brands, a channel, your app, a client or a prospect.
- Brand: one of your own projects. Brands are set up as an "Internal Brand", a "Content Channel" or an "App Build".
- Client: a business you do work for. Clients are scored on the client weekly scorecard.
- Prospect: a business you might pitch. Prospects live on the Prospect Dock, not in the project picker.
- Project type: the kind of project. It decides which pillars the project is scored on.
- "All projects": no single project is chosen, so Today shows the lists across every active brand.
- Active and Parked: a parked brand stays on Portfolio but drops out of Today's lists, and the crews skip it.
- Brand type: the kinds of brand a brand is, such as "Creator (personal brand)", "Content brand" or "Local news". It decides which setup items are standard for that brand.

## Today and your to-dos

- "Next 10": your ranked list of next steps. Today shows it across all projects, and each brand's Overview shows its own.
- "Waiting on me": suggested steps waiting for your yes or no. Approving one moves it into "Next 10".
- "Waiting on you": the count on a brand card and its Overview of open steps that are not Claude's, plus content ready for your approval.
- Action item: a to-do that feeds "Next 10" and "Waiting on me". Every pipeline step is an action item.
- Task: an item on the "Tasks" page. Tasks are a separate list and never feed "Next 10".
- P1 to P4: priority, with P1 the most urgent.
- "Snooze": hide an item for seven days.
- Capture: save a quick note to the Capture Inbox without sorting it.

## Scores

- Overall score: the weighted average of a project's pillar scores for one week, from 0 to 100.
- Pillar: one scored area of a project, such as "Audience" or "Revenue". Each pillar has a weight.
- KPI: a weekly number with a target. Pillars with KPIs are scored from them.
- Coverage line: how much of the score was actually measured, such as "2 of 5 pillars measured".
- Reporting week: the last completed Monday to Sunday week with numbers. Portfolio and the brand Overview show this week.
- WoW: week over week, the change from the week before.
- Grade: the letter that goes with a score. A is 90 and up, B 80 to 89, C 70 to 79, D 60 to 69, and F below 60.
- Setup score: how finished a brand's setup is, from 0 to 100. More important items count more, an account that needs access counts half, and skipped items do not count.
- "Ready to grow", "Caution" and "Set up first": the setup gate words, for a Setup score of 70 or more, 50 to 69, and under 50.
- "Potential": the share of a brand's money lanes that are switched on, weighted by importance.
- Growth score: a client's Growth Audit score.

## The setup map

- Asset: anything a brand needs set up, such as a social account, a store, a money account or its brand kit.
- "To set up": needed and not created yet.
- "Needs attention": accounts that exist but need access, or that are planned but not created.
- "Exists, needs access": the account exists, but you cannot get into it yet.
- "Planned, not set up": HQ has a record of the account, but it has not been created.
- "In place": done and recorded.
- "Later": put off for now. It still counts against the Setup score.
- "Skipped": ruled out for this brand, with a reason. It no longer counts.
- "More options": assets that are optional for this kind of brand, which you can add to its plan.
- "Foundation": the brand kit itself. It is filled in on the Brand Kit page.
- Setup brief: the plain-text list of a brand's gaps that "Copy setup brief" puts on your clipboard, for you or an assistant to follow.

## Channels

- Channel: one account, site or store a brand uses, such as an Instagram account or an Etsy shop.
- Handle: the account's name on the platform, usually starting with @.
- "Live", "Dormant", "Empty", "Planned" and "Needs access": a channel's status. Live is active, Dormant has gone quiet, Empty has no content yet, Planned is not created yet, and Needs access exists but cannot be reached.
- "STALE": the channel has a posting target and has gone too long without a post.
- "Method": the platform playbook that governs the channel.
- Level: a channel's or a job's content level, from 1 to 3.
- Number sources: "from a screenshot", "entered by hand", "from Metricool", "from the public page" and "from the platform" say where a set of numbers came from.

## Pipelines and steps

- Pipeline, or production job: the step-by-step plan for making one thing for one brand.
- Recipe: the playbooks a job was built from, with their versions.
- Step owners: "You" for your steps and "Claude" for Claude's. "Agent" and "Tool" mark steps owned by other automations.
- Step words: "Done" is finished; "Ready" is your step, free to do now; "Blocked" waits on an earlier step; "Awaiting your decision" is your step that starts with "Decision"; "Queued" is a Claude step waiting for the nightly run; "Open" is an agent or tool step that is not done.
- Job words: "Ready", "Running", "Blocked", "Waiting on a decision", "In review", "Finished" and "Parked". The database sets them as the job moves.
- "Current step": the step the job is on now.
- "Hand to Claude": give one of your steps to Claude.
- "Copy prompt": copy a ready-made prompt so Claude can do a step in a chat right away.
- Quality gate: the step where a content piece's quality is checked and the piece is approved.
- Money destination: where the money from a job is meant to land.
- "Method not written yet": the step's playbook is still planned. The nightly crew writes it first.
- "Make this": the button that turns a brief, a pain or a product idea into a pipeline.
- Nightly run: the production crew that runs at 2:13 AM Pacific.

## Content

- Content piece: one thing to publish, such as a video, a post or an article. HQ also calls it a content item.
- Stage: where a piece is in production: "Idea", "Script", "Design", "Record", "Edit", "Thumbnail", "Scheduled" and "Published".
- Format: the shape of a piece, such as "Long-form", "Short", "Reel", "Carousel", "Pin", "Post", "Story", "Article", "Email" or "Printable".
- Purpose: what a piece is for: teach, entertain, story, community, proof or offer.
- Placement: one piece on one channel and account, with its own status.
- Family: pieces made from the same source, such as one article turned into several posts.
- Idea score: how an idea scored in research, before it was made.
- Quality score: how a finished piece scored against the quality standard. Approval needs 80 or more.
- Hard fail: a rule a piece breaks that blocks approval whatever its score.
- "Ready to approve": a quality score of 80 or more, no hard fails, and no approval yet.
- "Approve batch": approve every ready piece on a brand's Content tab at once.
- Content mode: how much content HQ produces for a brand: "Growth", "Baseline" or "Parked".
- Hook: the opening line or angle meant to grab attention.
- Primary keyword: the main search phrase a piece aims at.

## Research and playbooks

- Brief: a ready-to-make idea filed by research, with its evidence.
- Niche profile: the research summary of who a brand's audience is and what they want.
- Pain: a problem, worry or question the audience has, in their own words.
- Pain score: how much a pain hurts, from 0 to 25.
- Playbook: the written method for one way of making, publishing, earning or promoting.
- Shelves: the playbook groups "Formats", "Platforms", "Money paths", "Niches", "Promotion", "Rules" and "Plays".
- "Written", "Thin" and "Planned": a playbook that is complete and current, one that is short or past its review date, and one that is not written yet.
- Superseded and Archived: an older version that a newer one replaced, and a retired playbook.
- Version: a playbook's version number, such as v1.0. Every change makes a new version.
- Review by: the date a playbook should be checked again. After it passes, the playbook shows as Thin.
- Sources: what fed a playbook. Your material shows "New", "Assigned", "Merged into" a version, or "Rejected:" with the reason.
- Play: a proven combination of format, platform and money path that clearly won, with the proof.
- "Core" and "Angle": whether a niche is central to a brand or reached from one side.

## Money

- Avenue, or money lane: a way a brand earns, such as an Etsy shop, Amazon KDP books or YouTube.
- "On", "Not on yet" and "Unknown": whether an avenue is set up.
- Severity: how much an avenue or finding matters, from "Low" to "Critical".
- "per hour": an avenue's estimated money per hour of your time.
- Product: something a brand sells, such as a printable, a planner or a book.
- Funnel stage: a product's role on the path from free to paid, such as a lead magnet.
- Outlet: a place a product is listed, such as Etsy, Amazon KDP, Gumroad or Payhip.
- Offer: a service package you sell to clients.
- Affiliate program: a program that pays you for sales you send.

## Clients and leads

- Deal stages: "Lead", "Pitch", "Proposal", "Won" and "Lost", in that order.
- Promoted: a lead that has become a CRM company or a project.
- Approval gate: no drafted customer message goes out until someone approves it.
- Finding: one problem or opportunity a Growth Audit detected.
- Action pack: drafted marketing pieces for one finding.
- Readiness gate: a check meant to stop you sending customers to a business that cannot handle them, with the states "Green Light", "Caution" and "Needs Ops Fix First".
- Leak: one way a business loses money, with its own formula.
- Recovery report: a weekly draft of what HQ helped a client win back. Its statuses are draft, reviewed and sent.

## System

- Admin: an account that can see and change everything, including the admin-only screens.
- Roles: "Owner", "General Manager", "Lead", "Producer" and "Contributor". A role decides which screens a staff account sees.
- "Preview Role": see HQ the way another role sees it. "Exit Preview" brings you back.
- Archive: hide a record but keep it and everything tied to it. Delete removes it for good.
- Crew: a scheduled Claude run, such as the nightly production run or the monthly Radar run.

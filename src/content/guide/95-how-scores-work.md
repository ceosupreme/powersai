# How scores work
<!-- part: Reference | order: 95 | slug: how-scores-work | route: /portfolio | tags: score, overall score, pillars, weights, setup score, ready to grow, caution, set up first, kpi, targets, grade, reporting week -->

Every brand and project in HQ has an overall score from 0 to 100. It is a weighted average of a few pillar scores, and each kind of project has its own pillars. This article explains where every number comes from, which week you are looking at, and what the Setup score and its three words mean.

## The overall score

- The overall score is the weighted average of the project's pillar scores for one week.
- Only pillars that have a number that week count. A pillar with no number is left out, not counted as zero. So early on, an overall score can rest on just one or two pillars. The coverage line near the score tells you how much was actually measured, for example "2 of 5 pillars measured" or "4 of 9 KPIs measured", with the week it covers and when the numbers were last updated.
- On a brand's Overview tab the box is called "Overall (weighted)". On Portfolio cards it is the big number on each card, with its change from the week before.
- Every score also gets a letter grade: A is 90 or more, B is 80 to 89, C is 70 to 79, D is 60 to 69, and F is below 60. Portfolio cards and pillar boxes show the letter next to the number.

## The pillars for each kind of project

Each project has a type, and the type decides its pillars and their weights.

- Content Channel: "Output", "Audience", "Engagement", "Monetization" and "Setup", 20 percent each.
- Internal Brand: "Growth", "Revenue", "Brand Presence", "Operations" and "Setup", 20 percent each.
- App Build: "Progress", "Quality", "Adoption", "Roadmap Health" and "Setup", 20 percent each.
- Client: "Revenue", "Team", "Delivery" and "Client Experience", 25 percent each. On Portfolio, client cards show these four as R, L, O and G, for Revenue, Labor, Operations and Guest, the names from the original bar scorecard.
- Service Offer: "Reach" 33 percent, "Conversion" 33 percent and "Delivery" 34 percent.
- Home Services, Plumbing and HVAC: "Demand Capture" 30 percent, "Sales & Estimates" 25 percent, "Capacity & Dispatch" 20 percent, "Retention & Membership" 15 percent and "Reputation" 10 percent.

The pillars and weights for a type, or a custom set for one single project, are managed in the Admin Panel. A custom set for a project replaces the type's set completely.

## Where a pillar score comes from

A pillar gets its number in one of three ways.

### From KPIs, when the pillar has them

1. Each KPI has a target, for example 4 videos a week or 500 new followers a month.
2. You enter the actual number for the week on the brand's pillar cards, or when a "Next 10" step asks for it.
3. HQ turns each KPI into a score from 0 to 100. When higher is better, the score is the actual divided by the target, capped at 100. When lower is better, such as a cost or a response time, it is the target divided by the actual. Checklist KPIs use their own 0 to 100 value.
4. The pillar score is the weighted average of its KPI scores. A KPI with no number or no target is skipped.

### By hand, when the pillar has no KPIs

On the brand's pillar card you can type a score from 0 to 100 for the week, with an optional note. When a pillar has KPIs, the KPI math always wins over a hand-typed score.

### Automatically, for Setup

The Setup pillar is filled in every night by HQ itself from the setup map. You never type it.

## The Setup score

The Setup score measures how finished a brand's setup is: whether it has the accounts, channels, money accounts, website and other assets a brand of its kind should have.

- HQ lists every asset the brand needs, based on what kind of brand it is, its niches and the money lanes it uses.
- Each needed asset has a priority. More important assets weigh more.
- An asset that is on file counts fully. One that exists but needs access counts as half. One that is missing, planned or put off with "Later" counts as nothing.
- An asset you chose to "Skip" is taken out of the count completely.
- The score is the weighted share of what is in place, from 0 to 100.

### The three words

- "Ready to grow": 70 or more. The brand is set up well enough that more promotion and content will pay off.
- "Caution": 50 to 69. Growth work can start, but the gaps will cost you.
- "Set up first": below 50. Finish the setup before spending on growth.

The Setup tab shows the live score and the word for the brand. The Setup pillar counts for 20 percent of the overall score for brands, channels and apps.

## Which week you are looking at

- Weeks run Monday to Sunday.
- Portfolio and the brand Overview show the last completed week that has numbers, not the week in progress. That keeps half-finished weeks from dragging scores down. A brand with no earlier week shows the current week instead.
- The Setup score is saved to the current week every night. On Portfolio it becomes part of the overall score once that week is complete. The Setup tab always shows today's number.

## Other numbers you will see next to scores

- "Potential" on a Portfolio card comes from the brand's money lanes on the Foundation Audit. It is not part of the overall score.
- Client businesses also have a Growth Audit score with its own gate, which says whether a business is ready to boost its marketing. See the Growth Audit article.

## How to raise a score

1. Open the brand and look at its pillar cards on the "Overview" tab. The lowest pillar with a weight is usually the fastest win.
2. For Setup, open the "Setup" tab and work through "To set up", highest priority first. "Set up the top 3" starts the three most important ones at once.
3. Enter missing KPI numbers. A pillar with no number does not count at all, which can make a score look better or worse than it is.
4. Check that each KPI has a sensible target. A target that is too low makes a pillar look perfect.

## Good to know

- Choosing "Later" on a setup item does not lift the Setup score. Only "Skip" takes an item out, so only skip items the brand truly will never need.
- A sudden jump in the overall score often means a pillar got its first number that week, not that the brand changed overnight. Check the coverage line.
- Scores are a guide for where to look next, not a grade on you.
